Case study 03 · Partner Losses & Billing

Monthly billing with rules nobody has to re-explain

Franchise partners pay for losses above an allowance that changes with store age, operator changes and region, day by day. The engine computes it for every store and writes a plain-language explanation for every partner who asks.

Scope300+ partner stores
CadenceMonthly + weekly review
OutputFinance report + per-store dashboards
StackPython · SQL · openpyxl
The problem

Rules that lived in people's heads

The allowance schedule, a regional exception, board-approved special ceilings and one-off corrections were applied by hand in spreadsheets. Mistakes were hard to trace, and every partner complaint meant hours of digging.

Stores that changed operator mid-month had to be split correctly between the outgoing and incoming partner.

What partners pay

One formula, many moving parts

CHARGE = max(0, billable losses − ALLOWANCE) + alcohol + tobacco

Billable losses are expired and damaged goods in four fresh and ambient categories. Alcohol and tobacco are charged in full and never touch the allowance. Frozen and some internal categories are shown but never charged.

The allowance engine

Computed for every single day

Chained months

Month boundaries move

Month 1 runs from the operator's start date; each next month starts the day after the previous one ended. End-of-month dates are clamped (31 Jan + 1 month = 28/29 Feb).

Tiers

Grace, then step down

Taken-over stores: unlimited in month 1, then two lower tiers, then a permanent floor. New stores keep a longer grace. Two regions have their own schedule.

Daily sum

Allowance = Σ tier ÷ days in month

Only over the numeric days in the billing window. Losses on grace days are fully exempt and never use up the allowance of other days.

One taken-over store · allowance per day across four monthsIllustrative data

Operator started on the 10th. In the highlighted billing month: days 1–10 are grace (exempt), days 11–30 sit in tier 2, so the allowance is 20 days × tier ÷ 30.

Manual corrections, without touching code

One editable file, three layers

  • Exclusions: a wrong transaction is set to zero but stays visible in the report.
  • Supplementary allowance: a fixed amount added on top, only when the allowance is numeric.
  • Preferential allowance: a board decision that replaces the schedule entirely.
  • Special windows: fix a billing window without editing the HR export, which is regenerated every month.
  • Every correction can be pinned to one operator's row with a composite key, so a handover month never applies it to the wrong partner.
Guards

Refuse bad input, loudly

  • Lines with impossible quantities or values (a barcode typed as quantity) are removed and listed.
  • If several input files match, the most recent one is used and named in the console.
  • The billing month is taken from the data's own dates when the label disagrees, after an export once shifted every store into the next month.
  • Template files and sheets from the wrong year are ignored.

Per-store dashboard

Generated for every complaint

Day-by-day tier breakdown, losses by category and product, and what was charged, rebuilt automatically each time the report runs.

Self-check

Never a misleading breakdown

If a manual override makes the applied allowance differ from the schedule, the dashboard says "special allowance" instead of showing tiers that no longer add up.

Anomalies

Flagged before billing

Duplicated losses, single lines above a threshold and stores losing more than a tenth of their sales are listed for review every week.

Outcome. The monthly cycle runs in minutes instead of days, from live data, with a full audit trail. Every change to the rules is checked by regenerating all reports and dashboards before and after and comparing them cell by cell.

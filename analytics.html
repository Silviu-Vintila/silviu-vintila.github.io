Six full weeks to yesterday, by store, product, category and area manager, with a delta against each one's own history and an alert sheet ranked by severity.

Days of shelf life left at the moment of delivery, per supplier and product, as an offline HTML dashboard with an Excel twin.

New products

First sale and first receipt, units sent vs sold vs lost at cost, and each launch's share of its category, weekly and monthly.

Why the simulator is right

Averages hide the money

The obvious shortcut is to compare the average store's loss with the allowance. Because stores vary a lot, that underestimates what is recovered: a store far above the allowance pays, a store below it pays nothing, and the two do not cancel out.

right: mean( max(0, loss_store − allowance) )
wrong: max(0, mean(loss) − allowance)

The simulator applies the right version to every real store, then rolls monthly cohorts through the year with the old and the proposed rule side by side.

Built to be rerun

Small engineering that matters

  • Product filters pushed into SQL as an indexed temp table: one slow week went from about 8 minutes to under 20 seconds.
  • Every extract cached per week as Parquet, so a report can be rebuilt without a database connection.
  • Workbooks written to a temporary name and renamed at the end, after two simultaneous runs once corrupted a file.
  • Past one million rows, the daily detail moves to CSV and the sheet switches to weekly grain automatically.
  • Losses valued at purchase price with the price valid on that day; sales at net value.

Outcome. Recurring questions now have a report that answers them the same way every time: one command, one workbook or dashboard, numbers that match the billing engine because they share its rules.

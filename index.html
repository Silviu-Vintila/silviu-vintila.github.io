01 · Forecast & Replenishment

A daily push engine that thinks before it ships

300+ stores × ~140 fresh SKUs · runs every weekday · replaced a fragile Excel/VBA master

The problem

Fresh food was allocated by a large Excel model. It was slow, hard to change, and nobody could say why a store got 3 or 30 units. Short shelf life meant every wrong unit became waste or an empty shelf.

What I built

  • Demand per store and weekday, with buffers by store tier and covered-days logic (Friday covers the weekend).
  • Stock reconstructed from delivery lots: FIFO sales, expiry, write-offs, so the engine knows what is really on the shelf.
  • Anti-waste rule: never send more than the store can sell before expiry.
  • Supplier allocation: cut by equal coverage when stock is short, share surplus by sales velocity when it is long.
  • New stores and launches: geo-neighbours within 1.5 km, similar products, opening-day completion.
  • An ML forecast plugged in as the demand source, with automatic fallback when it is stale.

Outcome

Every quantity is explained column by column. A rule audit and a replayable regression test run on each change. A 3-week backtest of the ML switch showed fewer units shipped while more demand was covered.

02 · Supplier Orders

Order proposals that follow the supplier calendar

11 suppliers · two-week rolling plan · holiday and store-opening aware

The problem

Each supplier has its own order days, delivery days and coverage windows. Buyers guessed quantities a week ahead, with no view of stock at delivery or of next week's school holiday.

What I built

  • A calendar reader: order day → store delivery day → days covered, checked so every week is covered exactly once.
  • Day-by-day stock simulation to each delivery, with lot expiry and planned deliveries.
  • Forecast with store trend, a holiday factor learned from past school holidays, and new openings from their neighbours.
  • Rounding to case size and MOQ, with a per-store breakdown behind every supplier line.
  • A holiday analysis: indexed sales per store and category for every past vacation.

Outcome

One file each morning: what to order today, the 14-day plan, and why. Shipped with a manual and a slide deck for the team.

03 · Partner Losses & Billing

Monthly billing with rules nobody had to re-explain

300+ partner stores · tiered allowances · handover / takeover splits

The problem

Partners pay for losses above an allowance that depends on store age, agent changes and region, day by day. The old process took days of manual Excel work, and disputes were hard to answer.

What I built

  • A day-by-day allowance engine: grace periods, tiers by month since takeover, regional exceptions.
  • Manual corrections in one editable file: exclusions, board-approved ceilings, special windows.
  • Per-store dashboards generated automatically for every partner complaint.
  • A weekly evolution section for the Monday meeting: who crossed the ceiling, which categories pushed them, anomalies.
  • Purchase-price view of the same summary, and guards that refuse stale or wrong input files.

Outcome

The monthly report runs in minutes instead of days, and every partner question gets a day-by-day explanation. Dashboards were compared cell by cell before and after each change: identical where they should be.

04 · Assortment & Availability

Same planogram, very different stores: finding out why

store × product × day · shelf stock rebuilt from delivery lots · board deck in Romanian and English

The problem

Stores with the same planogram were assumed to behave the same. Nobody could say which products sat dead on a shelf, which were missing exactly where they sell best, or whether new launches added sales or only took them from similar products.

What I built

  • Daily on-shelf status rebuilt from delivery lots (FIFO sales, write-offs, expiry), validated against system stock counts.
  • Availability per product and store: days on shelf ÷ days in range, with estimated lost sales on empty days.
  • A sales index adjusted for store size, so a small store is not flagged just for being small.
  • Six automatic signals: dead shelf, waste above sales, N-times gaps between stores, format effects, concentrated losses, sells-but-missing.
  • New-launch tracking (sent vs sold vs wasted, week by week), a like-for-like cannibalization check, and the summer-holiday effect by store cluster (school, office, campus).

Outcome

One run produces a board-ready HTML dashboard and an Excel workbook in two languages. Every signal comes with the stores and products behind it, so the commercial team acts on a list, not on an average.

Tools around the engines

Long-life stock push

Splits a limited quantity of ambient stock across stores: need = daily sales × days to expiry − stock, capped by shelf space, best sellers first, never above what is available.

ML forecast backtest

Compared a gradient-boosting forecast with the weekday baseline, using only the forecast available each morning, then wired it in with a switch and a fallback.

Stock accuracy audit

Rebuilt shelf stock from delivery lots and compared it with the system, which exposed a double-counted day of sales in the inherited formula.

Sales & loss report with alerts

Six weeks of day × store × product data, weekly evolution per store, manager and category, and an alert sheet that ranks anomalies by severity.

Shelf life at delivery

How many days of shelf life each supplier delivery really arrived with, as an offline HTML dashboard and an Excel twin with native charts.

Supplier scorecard, year to date

Received, sold and lost per supplier, product and store, with loss as a share of sales and the month-by-month trend.

New-product board report

First sale, first receipt, units sent vs sold vs lost at purchase price, and each launch's share of its category, by week and month.

Store × product profiles

Eight weeks of history per store, product and weekday, labelled automatically: not selling, high waste, discount-driven, runs out, balanced.

Allowance policy simulator

Simulated next year's ceiling options on the real loss distribution to show the budget impact of each policy before it was decided.

Rule audit & regression harness

Records each weekday's SQL inputs once, replays the engine offline, and checks a dozen business rules on every output before it ships.

Year-to-date loss review

Month-by-month losses by store cluster and category, rebuilt from one query and the monthly billing files, with impossible lines (barcodes typed as quantity) caught and listed.

Report & slide automation

Excel workbooks with live formulas and charts, table-diff tools for allocation versions, and PowerPoint decks generated straight from the data.
